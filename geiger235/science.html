ATOMS

An atom is the fundamental unit of the universe and matter as a whole. There’s no atomic energy or bombs without these little guys.

Atoms are made of simple forces.

The Nucleus:
is the center, composed of 99.99% of all the matter of 1 atom, making up less than 1% of the total volume. It’s made of the Positive Protons and Neutral Neutrons.

The Cloud of Electrons:
around the nucleus are Negatively Charged. They allow atoms to bond and stay together.

Atoms need humongous amounts of energy to contain all these particles together.

ATOMIC RAYS

Radiation is in forms of non-ionizing radiation and ionizing radiation.

Non-ionizing:
Microwaves, radio waves, visible light.

Non-ionizing is everything from your microwaved dinner to the internet and digital tech uses these, letting the modern world be comfortable and entertained.

Ionizing:
X-rays, gamma-rays, UV.

Ionizing is stuff like bombs, power plants and medical tech use this heavy duty power.

ATOMIC ENERGY

The basic process behind the atomic energies we use in modern life are:

Nuclear Fission:

Producing energy from splitting apart atoms
Fission uses unstable, easy-to-split atoms like Uranium-235 (a rare isotope of Uranium). A low-power neutron is fired at a uranium atom, splits it, sends those neutrons flying to do the same thing. It’s a chain reaction that generates tons of heat energy to move things like a steam engine!

Nuclear Fusion:

Producing energy from making new atoms.
Fusion generates energy by fusing matter together. Hydrogen isotopes that repel each other are use fission power to fuse, generating more energy than the fission. This is seen in things like the sun and hydrogen bombs.

RAD ACTION

Characters like the Hulk and Spider-man were quintessential pop-culture superhero icons that defined the use of radiation in science fiction.

Though Flash (1956, Barry Allen) and Superman (1938, Clark Kent) solidified science fiction in comic book media, Radiation induced superpowers weren’t used in mainstream media until Stan Lee wrote the Hulk and Spider-man. They saw their radioactive debuts in American publications.

Spider-man (1962, Peter Parker) saw his powers manifest from a spider dosed with ambiguous ionized radiation all the way back in Amazing Fantasy #15.

Hulk (1962, Bruce Banner) saw his raging strength come from gamma radiation consuming his body mind and soul in The Incredible Hulk #1.

MONSTERS

Tomoyuki Tanaka, movie producer, conjured up the tale of Godzilla as a monster movie that cautioned against mankind’s tendency to destroy itself with radiation and warfare.

Godzilla is a simple creature. A behemoth lizard that breathes radioactive fire and smashes apart cities like a Fat Man bomb with arms and legs.

Godzilla has seen 39 movies alone as of writing this. His ability to keep pushing franchise materials is a testament to mankind’s worries about how radiation, bombs and nature intermingle.

Fallout: A Post Nuclear Role Playing Game

RAD GAMES

In 1997, Interplay studios produced Fallout, the post apocalyptic RPG.

This game combined the 1950s atomic age aesthetic with wasteland iconography to deconstruct optimistic attitudes about atomic progress, war, and technological idolatry. 

The game is set in an alternate earth where radiation based tech advanced fast in the United states, yet at the sacrifice of america’s integrity and benevolence. The post nuclear wasteland keeps reminding the player of the horrors of radiation.

You manage radiation along with your hunger and health, fight off radioactive mutants, and see cults form around technology and radiation.
